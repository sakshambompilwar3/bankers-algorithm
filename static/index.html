Operating Systems Theory & Deadlock Avoidance

1. What is Banker's Algorithm?

Developed by Edsger W. Dijkstra (1965), the Banker's Algorithm is a dynamic deadlock-avoidance algorithm. It tests for safety by simulating the allocation of predetermined maximum possible amounts of all resources, and then makes an "s-state" check to test for possible deadlocks before permitting actual allocation.

2. The Crucial Difference: Unsafe State vs. Deadlock

An Unsafe State is NOT necessarily a Deadlock!
An unsafe state simply means the operating system cannot guarantee that processes will avoid deadlock if every process simultaneously demands its maximum declared resources. The system may run to completion safely if applications complete earlier or request fewer resources, but deadlock avoidance defensively refuses to enter unsafe states.

3. The Four Coffman Deadlock Conditions

For a deadlock to occur, all four conditions must hold simultaneously:

  • Mutual Exclusion: At least one resource must be held in a non-shareable mode.
  • Hold and Wait: A process must be currently holding at least one resource and requesting additional resources.
  • No Preemption: Resources cannot be forcibly seized from a process; they can only be released voluntarily.
  • Circular Wait: A closed chain of processes exists such that each process holds at least one resource needed by the next.
